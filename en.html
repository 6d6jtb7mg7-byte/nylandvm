The question

Hypothesis: Ørjan Nyland may have produced one of the strongest complete goalkeeper tournaments at the 2026 World Cup. This is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion assumed in advance.

What the knockout data says

300 knockout minutes · 13 ordinary saves
That equals 3.90 saves per 90 minutes. The analysis also separates ordinary saves from high-leverage events such as a regulation penalty save and a documented late survival save.

Blind model

The comparison uses shot-stopping, high-leverage interventions, knockout pressure, distribution, sweeping/field work, errors and external validation. The resulting scores are model-dependent, not an objective official ranking.

Changing legitimate weights can change the ordering. That sensitivity is part of the point of the stress test.

What survives the stress test?

The evidence does not establish Nyland as the tournament's best pure shot-stopper. It does support a more moderate claim: he belongs in the discussion of the strongest complete goalkeeper performances, particularly when knockout impact and distribution are included.

Method and transparency

Facts, calculations and custom models are kept separate. Provider totals can differ because event definitions differ. External awards and ratings are context, not ground truth. The Norwegian version contains the full interactive data story and source discussion.
